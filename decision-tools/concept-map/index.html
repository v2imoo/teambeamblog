Decision Tools

Concept map

Diagram how ideas in a domain relate, with labelled links.

Contents

Overview

A concept map lays out the concepts in a domain as nodes and connects them with labelled arrows that name the relationship — ‘causes’, ‘is part of’, ‘depends on’. Unlike a mind map, which radiates from one centre, a concept map is a network, and the labels on the links carry as much meaning as the nodes.

It is a tool for understanding structure. Building the map forces you to articulate exactly how things relate, which surfaces gaps and misconceptions in your own understanding — the act of mapping is itself the learning.

When to use it

Learning or explaining a complex topic, or aligning a team on how parts of a domain fit together.

How to use it

Step 1

List the key concepts

Identify the important ideas in the domain.

Step 2

Rank rough hierarchy

Place broader concepts higher, more specific ones below.

Step 3

Connect with labelled links

Draw arrows and name each relationship explicitly.

Step 4

Add cross-links

Connect concepts in different branches to show how the domain interrelates.

Step 5

Revise as understanding grows

Treat the map as living — update it as you learn, and let gaps guide study.

Worked example

A new hire maps the company’s product domain: ‘users’ — create → ‘content’ — feeds → ‘recommendations’ — drive → ‘engagement’ — funds → ‘the business’. Building it, they realise they don’t understand how ‘moderation’ links in — an explicit gap that tells them exactly what to ask about next.

Common pitfalls

  • Leaving links unlabelled, which loses the meaning the map exists to capture.
  • Cramming in every concept until the map is noise.
  • Treating it as a one-off diagram rather than a living model.

Frequently asked questions

How is a concept map different from a mind map?

A mind map radiates from a single central idea; a concept map is a network with labelled relationships between many concepts.

Why label the links?

Because the relationship (‘causes’, ‘depends on’) is where much of the meaning lives — unlabelled arrows lose it.

Is it mainly for learning?

It is excellent for learning and for aligning a team on how a domain fits together.
