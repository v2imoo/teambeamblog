Decision Tools

OODA loop

Observe, Orient, Decide, Act — and cycle faster than the situation changes.

Contents

Overview

The OODA loop is a decision cycle built for speed under uncertainty: Observe the situation, Orient by making sense of it against your experience and context, Decide on a course, and Act — then loop, because your action has changed the situation. Boyd’s claim was that whoever cycles through this loop faster and more accurately controls the tempo and forces the opponent to react.

Orientation is the hidden heart of the loop. It is where raw observations get filtered through your mental models, culture and prior experience — and where two people seeing the same facts reach opposite conclusions. Sharpen orientation and the whole loop improves.

When to use it

Fast-moving, competitive or uncertain situations where waiting for full information means losing — crises, competition, negotiation.

How to use it

Step 1

Observe

Gather the raw, current signal — what is actually happening right now, not what you expected.

Step 2

Orient

Interpret it through your models and context. Update your picture; discard assumptions that no longer fit.

Step 3

Decide

Choose a course based on the current orientation, accepting it is provisional.

Step 4

Act

Do it — decisively — then observe how the world responded and loop again.

Worked example

A startup facing a bigger competitor cannot out-resource them, so it out-loops them: it ships a change weekly, watches how users respond, re-orients, and ships again. The incumbent, orienting through quarterly planning, is always reacting to last month. Speed of iteration, not size, decides who leads.

Common pitfalls

  • Getting stuck observing — collecting data instead of orienting and acting.
  • Orienting through stale mental models, so faster looping just repeats the same mistake faster.
  • Confusing speed with haste — a fast loop with bad orientation loses.

Frequently asked questions

Why is orientation the most important step?

Because it determines what your observations mean and which decisions even occur to you — it shapes the other three.

Is OODA only for the military?

No. It fits any fast, adversarial or uncertain domain — business, sport, negotiation, emergencies.

How do I loop faster?

Shorten the gap between acting and observing the result, and keep your orientation models current.
