Eisenhower Matrix

Sort tasks by importance and urgency so the important stops losing to the merely urgent.

Contents

Overview

The Eisenhower Matrix splits every task along two axes — how important it is and how urgent it is — producing four quadrants. Its entire purpose is to expose a trap: urgent things feel like they matter because they shout, while important things sit quietly and get postponed until they too become urgent (or become a crisis).

Most people spend their days in the urgent-and-important quadrant fighting fires, and in the urgent-but-unimportant quadrant answering other people’s priorities. The matrix’s real payload is the important-but-not-urgent quadrant — the planning, prevention and deep work that quietly determines whether your life has fewer fires next month.

When to use it

Your to-do list is long, everything feels urgent, and important-but-not-urgent work (strategy, health, relationships) keeps getting crowded out.

How to use it

Step 1

Do — urgent & important

Crises and hard deadlines. Handle these now, but treat a full quadrant as a warning that you are living reactively.

Step 2

Schedule — important, not urgent

The quadrant that decides your future. Give it a real slot in the calendar before urgency arrives.

Step 3

Delegate — urgent, not important

Someone else’s priority wearing a deadline. Hand it off wherever possible.

Step 4

Delete — neither

Busywork and distraction. Stop doing it, and feel no guilt.

Worked example

A founder lists twelve tasks. Eight are urgent emails (delegate or delete), two are firefights with a customer (do), one is “write the hiring plan” and one is “get the health check” — both important, neither urgent, both postponed for a month. The matrix makes visible that the two tasks that most shape the next year are the only two with no time booked. They get scheduled first.

Common pitfalls

  • Confusing urgent with important — the whole point is that they are different axes.
  • Never scheduling quadrant two, so it silently migrates into quadrant one as emergencies.
  • Over-categorising: the matrix is a fast triage, not an accounting system.

Frequently asked questions

How is importance different from urgency?

Urgency is about time — a deadline is near. Importance is about consequence — it changes an outcome you care about. Many urgent things are unimportant, and vice versa.

What should I do first?

Handle the true quadrant-one crises, then immediately protect time for quadrant two before the day fills up.

Why do important tasks get ignored?

They rarely have a deadline, so they lose every contest against tasks that do — until they become crises themselves.
